About

We started Meridian because too much AI spend goes nowhere.

We'd each spent years building and shipping large-scale systems inside major technology companies. Moving into consulting, we kept seeing the same pattern: strong teams, real budget, and pilots that never made it past the demo stage.

Our take on the gap

Industry surveys consistently put the failure rate of enterprise AI initiatives well above 80% — not because the models don't work, but because the surrounding decisions never get made: who owns the outcome, what "done" means, how the system gets monitored once it's live, and whether the economics actually hold up outside a demo.

Traditional consultancies are built to advise, not to carry a system into production. Traditional system integrators are built to build, not to challenge whether the use case is worth building at all. Both gaps leave the client holding the risk.

How we're structured differently

Meridian runs both halves of the job with the same team. The people who scope the opportunity and build the business case are the same people accountable for the system once it's live. That keeps us honest about what's actually achievable, and keeps momentum from stalling at the handoff between "strategy" and "execution."

  • One senior pod per engagement, not a rotating cast of consultants.
  • Fixed pricing agreed up front, with the mix of skills on the pod adapting as the work moves from strategy to delivery.
  • Explicit go/no-go gates between phases, so a bad bet gets killed early and cheaply rather than late and expensively.

Who we work with

We partner with enterprise and mid-market teams — usually inside financial services, healthcare, utilities, manufacturing, and logistics — who have real data, a genuine business problem, and the internal sponsorship to see a system through to production. If any of those three is missing, we'll tell you that during Discover rather than after the invoice.
